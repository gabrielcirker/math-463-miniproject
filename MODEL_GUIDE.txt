immune model notes

what this is
this code studies how conventional T cells and regulatory T cells interact with IL-2
it also checks whether equations can be learned from the simulated data
this is a general immune model with no myelin injury or recovery variable
the results are model results rather than patient measurements

the three variables
T means activated conventional T cells
R means activated regulatory T cells or Tregs
I means interleukin 2 or IL-2 which is a growth signal used by both cell groups
I does not mean infection here

the equations
dT/dt = a*I*T - b*T - c*T^2 - gamma*R*T + k
dR/dt = epsilon*a*I*R - b*R + lambda*k
dI/dt = d*T - e*I*(T+R) - f*I

what the terms do
a*I*T lets IL-2 help conventional T cells multiply
b*T accounts for their natural loss
c*T^2 limits their growth through activation induced cell death called fratricide
gamma*R*T represents suppression by Tregs
k supplies newly activated conventional T cells at a constant rate
epsilon*a*I*R lets IL-2 help Tregs multiply
b*R accounts for natural Treg loss
lambda*k supplies newly activated Tregs
d*T represents conventional T cells making IL-2
e*I*T and e*I*R represent both cell groups using IL-2
f*I represents IL-2 breaking down
Tregs use IL-2 but do not make it in this model

the default setup
a = 0.4
b = 0.1
c = 1e-5
d = 0.01
e = 0.01
f = 1
gamma = 0.1
epsilon = 0.6
k = 1.4
lambda = 0.006
the baseline starts with T = R = I = 0
time uses general model units rather than days
lambda is a ratio of renewal rates rather than a measured Treg percentage

why k stays constant
the full paper also includes naive conventional cells N and resting Tregs Nhat
their activation rates are beta*N and beta*Nhat
when those resting pools are at equilibrium the paper gives
k = beta*N0/(g+beta)
khat = lambda*k
this code replaces those changing supplies with constant inputs
that leaves the three equations above rather than the full five variable model

Table 1 prints an unclear value B for g so the code does not assign it a value
Table 1 also gives N0 = 4 which makes k less than 4 in that equilibrium formula
the Figure 5 comparison includes k = 8.5 as an independently supplied input
that case cannot come from N0 = 4 without changing the resting pool assumptions
the baseline k = 1.4 is a separate case
this is why the outputs describe a constant input interpretation of the paper

comparison with the published time series
the six Figure 5 cases show T over time at different values of k and lambda
the baseline plot shows all three variables together
the paper proposes that low stimulation can give a weak response
moderate stimulation can support a large response with repeated oscillations
strong stimulation can activate enough Tregs to suppress the response
there is no antigen variable here because stimulation is supplied to the model
matching a plot visually does not establish an exact match to the authors data
the baseline inset was also checked against its printed blue trace
PAPER_COMPARISON.txt records the pixel calibration and scope of that comparison

parameter experiment
the experiment compares lambda = 0.006  0.009  0.012  0.02 while holding k at 1.4
the results record peak T and how the later response changes
later measurements use the final 75 percent of the trajectory
peak spacing is a descriptive estimate rather than proof of a stable cycle
check the number of peak intervals before interpreting that estimate

learning equations with SINDy
SINDy means sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics
it tries to learn equations using a small number of candidate terms
the baseline training windows end at 50  150  300
the lambda = 0.02 case uses a window ending at 300
validation starts at T = 25  R = 0.25  I = 0.10 and runs for 120 time units
testing starts at T = 50  R = 0.5  I = 0.15 and runs from 0 to 600
validation is extra data used to choose settings rather than refit coefficients
the test trajectory is kept out of that choice

how the fitting works
fourth order finite differences estimate derivatives from the sampled states
the fit does not get the true equations to calculate those training derivatives
training data set the state scales and the candidate term scales
root mean square scaling keeps large and small quantities more comparable
STLSQ means sequentially thresholded least squares
it fits coefficients removes small terms and refits the terms that remain
settings are ranked by mean relative derivative error across the three equations
each equation error is divided by its validation derivative mean square
the score also adds 1e-5 for each retained coefficient
a candidate must finish a nonnegative validation forecast to qualify
the true coefficients and test errors do not choose the settings

how to read the output
derivative error measures agreement with estimated rates of change
forecast error measures agreement after integrating the learned equations
NRMSE means normalized root mean square error
here it is the forecast root mean square error divided by that states true test range
check each state separately and compare the coefficients as well as the curves
small forecast errors do not guarantee that every correct term was recovered
a failed or negative forecast remains a failure in the output
short data windows and nearly constant states can make terms hard to distinguish
we use clean simulated data and include the correct quadratic terms so this is an easier case for SINDy
